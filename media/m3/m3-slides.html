Physics model: thermal oxidation

Calibrate the Deal-Grove model on the furnace, give its uncertainty, and say where it applies.

SemiAcademy · ISEP-CSUCC BY 4.0

By the end of this module you can

  • explain the Deal-Grove model x² + A·x = B·(t + τ): the linear and parabolic rate constants, and τ for a starting oxide;
  • describe how B and B/A depend on temperature (Arrhenius) and on the ambient, dry or wet;
  • fit the model to thickness measurements with the uncertainty on the thickness, and read the parameter uncertainties;
  • tell a confidence band for the predicted mean from a prediction band for a new measurement;
  • state the model's range of validity, including the thin-oxide regime, and the risk of extrapolation.

This is course outcome 3. The lab calibrates the model for your own simulated furnace.

How dry oxide grows

Deal and Grove (1965) set the three fluxes equal in steady state. The oxide grows at the silicon interface, so each nanometre of oxide consumes about 0.44 nm of silicon.

The Deal-Grove model

x² + A·x = B·(t + τ)  ⇒  x(t) = (A/2)·(√(1 + 4B(t + τ)/A²) − 1)
SymbolNameTextbook value, dry O₂, 1000 °C, (100) Si
Bparabolic rate constant174.0 nm²/min
B/Alinear rate constant0.748 nm/min
AB divided by B/A233 nm
τtime shift for a starting oxide x_i: τ = (x_i² + A·x_i)/B37.0 min for x_i = 25 nm

Deal and Grove, J. Appl. Phys. 36, 3770 (1965): B = 772 µm²/h · e^(−1.23 eV/kT); B/A = 6.23×10⁶ µm/h · e^(−2.0 eV/kT) for (111), divided by 1.68 for (100).

Two limits

  • Thin oxide (x ≪ A ≈ 233 nm): the reaction limits the rate, and x ≈ (B/A)·t.
  • Thick oxide (x ≫ A): diffusion through the oxide limits it, and x ≈ √(B·t).
  • The full solution moves smoothly from one to the other.
  • A bare wafer at 1000 °C grows about 69 nm in 2 hours (textbook constants).
Module 3 · Physics model: thermal oxidation5

A starting oxide: the time shift τ

  • A wafer that already has x_i of oxide behaves as if it had been oxidizing for τ minutes already.
  • τ = (x_i² + A·x_i)/B = 37.0 min for 25 nm.
  • The same idea answers a two-step recipe: find the equivalent time of the oxide grown so far, then add the new time.
  • τ is computed from the known x_i, not fitted.

Pause and predict

How thick after 2 hours?

A wafer starts with 25 nm of oxide and is oxidized for 120 minutes in dry O₂ at 1000 °C, with the textbook constants: B = 174.0 nm²/min, B/A = 0.748 nm/min.

Predict: more or less than 25 + 120 × (B/A) = 115 nm?

Less: x = 85.8 nm. With τ = 37.0 min, x = (A/2)(√(1 + 4B(120 + τ)/A²) − 1). The linear estimate ignores that diffusion through the growing oxide already slows the rate at 25 to 90 nm, because A is only 233 nm.
Module 3 · Physics model: thermal oxidation7

Temperature and ambient

  • B and B/A follow Arrhenius laws, C·e^(−E/kT): B with 1.23 eV, B/A with 2.0 eV (dry).
  • From 1000 to 1100 °C, B grows by a factor of 2.26.
  • Wet (H₂O) oxidation is far faster: in 2 hours at 1000 °C, about 617 nm from a 25 nm start, against 86 nm dry.
  • The lab uses one temperature only: 1000 °C, dry.
Module 3 · Physics model: thermal oxidation8
Pause and predict

How much faster at 1100 °C?

B = C₁·e^(−E₁/kT) with E₁ = 1.23 eV and k = 8.617×10⁻⁵ eV/K.

Predict: by what factor does B grow from 1000 °C (1273 K) to 1100 °C (1373 K)?

B(1100)/B(1000) = exp[(1.23/k)(1/1273.15 − 1/1373.15)] = 2.26. A 100-degree change more than doubles the parabolic rate, which is why a furnace's temperature calibration matters so much to its oxide thickness.

Calibration data from the furnace

  • 12 runs, 30 to 480 minutes, dry O₂ at 1000 °C, all with a measured 25.0 nm starting oxide.
  • One ellipsometer measurement per run; the metrology group states σ = 0.5 nm.
  • The textbook curve is close, but not right for this furnace.
  • Simulated data, example key 2026.
Module 3 · Physics model: thermal oxidation10

A straight line, for starting values only

t = (x² − x_i²)·(1/B) + (x − x_i)·(1/(B/A))
  • Linear in 1/B and 1/(B/A): ordinary least squares on t gives starting values. Three runs: B = 167.5 nm²/min, B/A = 0.7879 nm/min.
  • But least squares assumes the noise is in t. Here t is set exactly by the recipe and the noise is in x, which sits on the right, squared.
  • That is an errors-in-variables problem: the straight-line fit gives no honest uncertainty. Fit x(t) instead.

Fit x(t), with the noise on the thickness

curve_fit(model, t, x, p0,
  sigma=0.5 nm for every x,
  absolute_sigma=True)

Free parameters: B and B/A only. τ follows from x_i.

The two estimates are correlated

  • Correlation -0.97: a larger B with a smaller B/A fits almost as well.
  • Each standard error alone hides that: the joint uncertainty is a narrow, tilted ellipse.
  • Predictions need the full covariance, not two separate error bars.
  • curve_fit returns the covariance from a linear approximation at the optimum.
Module 3 · Physics model: thermal oxidation13

Does the model describe the data?

  • χ² = 7.5 for 10 degrees of freedom: about what noise of σ = 0.5 nm would give.
  • Residuals within ±2σ, with no trend in time.
  • So, inside 30 to 480 minutes, the model fits to within the measurement noise.
  • That says nothing yet about other conditions.

Confidence band and prediction band

  • Predicted at 270 min: 143.41 nm.
  • Confidence band (the mean): ± 0.33 nm, from the parameter covariance.
  • Prediction band (one new wafer): ± 1.03 nm, with se_pred = √(se_mean² + σ²).
  • The new run's three wafers fall inside the prediction band.
Module 3 · Physics model: thermal oxidation15
Pause and predict

How wide is the prediction band?

A calibrated model gives se_mean = 0.30 nm at some time, and one measurement has σ = 0.50 nm.

Predict: the half-width of the 95% prediction band?

se_pred = √(0.30² + 0.50²) = 0.583 nm, so the half-width is 1.96 × 0.583 = 1.14 nm. Calibrating with more runs shrinks se_mean, but never below the measurement's own σ.
Module 3 · Physics model: thermal oxidation16

Extrapolation

Beyond 480 minutes the band widens, and it still assumes that the model is right out there. Nothing in 30 to 480 minutes tests that.

Module 3 · Physics model: thermal oxidation17

A challenging condition: thin oxide

  • Real dry oxidation is faster than Deal-Grove below about 20 nm (Massoud, Plummer and Irene, 1985).
  • The course's simulator includes such extra early growth: this mismatch is built in on purpose.
  • Bare wafers, 10 to 40 min: every wafer is 8 to 9 prediction standard errors above the prediction.
  • The bands could not warn: the calibration never tested thin oxide.
Module 3 · Physics model: thermal oxidation18

State where the model applies

  • This calibration: dry O₂, 1000 °C, a starting oxide near 25 nm, 30 to 480 minutes, (100) wafers.
  • Outside it: other temperatures, wet ambient, thin oxides, long runs. The twin should flag the prediction as unsupported, or use a model built for the condition.
  • Bands describe noise and parameter uncertainty, not a wrong model.
  • Claim only what the data support: on simulated data, a good fit shows the method recovers the course's simulator, not that a real furnace follows Deal-Grove.
Module 3 · Physics model: thermal oxidation19

Summary

  • x² + A·x = B·(t + τ): linear growth for thin oxide, parabolic for thick, τ for the oxide already there.
  • B and B/A rise steeply with temperature; wet oxidation is much faster than dry.
  • Fit x(t) with σ on the thickness and absolute_sigma=True; B and B/A are strongly correlated.
  • Confidence band: the mean. Prediction band: one new measurement, wider by σ.
  • The bands widen on extrapolation and say nothing about a wrong model: test a challenging condition.
