Module 1 · What a digital twin is2

Two definitions

Digital Twin Consortium

A virtual representation of real-world entities and processes, driven by data, with “synchronized interaction at a specified frequency and fidelity.”

digitaltwinconsortium.org, definition first published in 2020

NIST IR 8356 (2025)

“A digital twin is the virtual (i.e., digital) representation of a physical or perceived real-world entity, concept, or notion.”

Voas, Mell, Laplante and Piroumian, Security and Trust Considerations for Digital Twin Technology

Both start from a virtual representation. The Consortium adds what makes it a twin rather than a model: synchronization, at a frequency and fidelity chosen for the use.

Pause and predict

Model, shadow or twin?

An engineer keeps a spreadsheet model of the furnace. At the end of each shift she types in the shift's average zone temperatures, and if the predicted oxide thickness looks off, she changes the recipe by hand.

Predict: where does it sit in the classification?

A digital model. Data reach the spreadsheet only when a person types them in, and changes reach the furnace only by hand: the data flow is manual in both directions. It can still be useful; the classification says how it is connected, not how good it is.

Three levels of coupling

LevelWhat the twin doesIn a fab
1updates itself from observationsthe furnace model follows each thermocouple reading
2its predictions support a person's decisionan engineer sees a thickness forecast and adjusts the recipe
3a controller acts on its predictions automaticallyrun-to-run control sets the next oxidation time within limits

Real systems mix levels, and most keep a person in the loop. This course uses the levels instead of one strict line between a shadow and a twin. The lab reaches level 1; Module 5 adds levels 2 and 3.

Fidelity and update frequency

Fidelity

How much of the real system the twin represents, and how accurately: one zone temperature, or every zone, gas flow and wafer. The Consortium's view: enough for the use, and no more.

Update frequency

How often the twin is synchronized: every reading, every run, every night. It must be fast enough for how quickly the real system changes and how large an error the use can accept.

The Try it explorer and Step 6 of the lab show the cost of a slow update: the twin drifts with its model between updates.

A first model of the furnace zone

T(t) = Tset − (Tset − T0) e−t/τ
  • The gap to the setpoint shrinks by the same fraction every second.
  • After τ: 63% of the way; after 3τ: 95%.
  • T0 = 800 °C, Tset = 1000 °C; τ = 150 s here.

Pause and predict

How long to get within 10 °C?

With τ = 150 s, the zone starts 200 °C below the setpoint. The gap shrinks as e−t/τ.

Predict: how long until the zone is within 10 °C of 1000 °C?

The gap must shrink by a factor of 200 / 10 = 20, so t = τ · ln 20 = 150 × 3.00 ≈ 449 s, about 7.5 minutes, or 3τ. A twin that knows τ can answer this before the run; that is one use of a model.

Fitting τ to the first 10 minutes

fit_tau(t, y, T0, T_set) finds the τ that brings the model closest to the readings (least squares, SciPy).

Example stream: τ = 130.76 ± 0.14 s.

The simulation's real τ is 130.78 s: within one standard error. A real furnace never tells you this.

The model alone falls behind

After about 15 minutes, something the model does not know about cools the zone. RMS error: 0.37 °C before 600 s, 5.61 °C from 600 s on, and up to 13.8 °C.

Module 1 · What a digital twin is13

From model to twin: correct with each reading

Tnew = Tpred + gain · (reading − Tpred)
  • gain 0: ignore the reading; gain 1: jump to it.
  • A missing reading (NaN) teaches nothing: keep Tpred.
  • Data flow one way, furnace to twin: a digital shadow, level 1.

Pause and predict

Does syncing always help?

The synced twin (gain 0.5) and the model alone use the same fitted τ = 130.76 s.

Predict: before 600 s, when the model is right, is the synced twin's error smaller or larger than the model's? And after the dip starts?

Before 600 s it is slightly larger: 0.41 against 0.37 °C, because each correction carries some noise from the reading into the state. From 600 s on it is much smaller: 1.29 against 5.61 °C, because the readings show the twin what the model cannot know.
Module 1 · What a digital twin is15

How often should the twin update?

Between updates, the twin runs on its model alone.

Updating every 10 s keeps the error at 1.29 °C; every 3 minutes, 4.95 °C, close to the model alone.

The right frequency depends on how fast the furnace changes and on the error the use can accept.

Claim only what the data support

  • On simulated data, a good fit shows that the method recovers the course's simulator; it does not validate a model of a real furnace.
  • With real data, check the residuals and test the model on data it was not fitted to.
  • A first-order model fits the warm-up of this simulated zone; a real zone has several heaters, a controller and a load.
  • Say what your twin is for, how it is connected, and how often it updates.

Summary

  • A digital twin is a virtual representation kept in step with a real entity at a chosen frequency and fidelity.
  • Model, shadow, twin: manual or automatic data flow, in one direction or both.
  • Levels of coupling: updates from observations, supports a decision, acts automatically.
  • First-order furnace model: fit τ; correct with each reading using a gain.
